There’s a moment, early in every project, where someone asks for “a bit more delight.” It’s a trap. Delight isn’t a layer you spray on at the end — it’s a byproduct of motion that *means something*.

The fastest way to make an interface feel cheap is to animate for animation’s sake. The fastest way to make it feel expensive is to make every transition carry information.

## Motion answers three questions

Every animation worth shipping answers at least one of these:

- **Where did this come from?** A panel that slides in from the edge it’s anchored to.
- **Where did it go?** A deleted row that collapses rather than blinking out.
- **What’s related to what?** Shared-element transitions that carry an object across routes.

If a motion can’t answer one of those, it’s decoration. Decoration has a budget, and that budget is small.

> The goal isn’t motion the user notices. It’s motion that means they never have to think.

## Physics over duration

Easing curves are fine. Springs are better. A `duration: 300ms` animation always takes 300ms, no matter how far the object travels. A spring takes as long as the *distance* demands — which is exactly how the physical world behaves, and exactly what our intuition expects.

```ts
// Distance-aware, interruptible, and it just feels right.
const transition = {
  type: 'spring',
  stiffness: 380,
  damping: 30,
  mass: 0.6,
};
```

The bonus: springs are interruptible. Click again mid-animation and the motion redirects from its current velocity instead of snapping or queueing. That responsiveness is most of what “premium” feels like.

## Respect the people who opt out

None of this matters if it makes someone sick. `prefers-reduced-motion` isn’t an edge case — it’s a contract. Honour it by removing *transforms and parallax*, not by removing *feedback*. A reduced-motion user should still know their click registered; they just shouldn’t have to watch the world slide.

Motion is a language. Spend your words carefully, and the interface starts to feel less like software and more like a place.
